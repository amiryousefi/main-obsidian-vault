## **Your Brand’s Content Narrative**

This is everything and sets the tone for the series you develop. Your narrative is the through line. And it’s the overarching story you want to amplify as a brand to develop a specific perception.

It’s key to know this, understand this, and use this as the *north star* in your content strategy. Because if not, the show you create can build the wrong perception, even if it’s performing “good.”

**That narrative is the intersection of:**

1. Your Mission
2. What You Believe
3. What People Will Rally Behind

Then put it into 3-5 words.

But the next step is establishing the feelings/emotions your content needs to evoke while creating brand filters for all content ideas.

![[G-4HZj3XAAEeG2t.png]]

Next is…

## **The Importance Of Content Buckets**

This is making the right content for the right person.

Which makes your content buckets your top customer segments. Because with the right content, you can target specific audiences. There’s a reason if you consume fashion content, your explore feed will be fashion content. And the same goes for any category.

Algos learn what you love to consume and show you more of it.

So, as a brand, you can create a show, based on your top customer segments, who they are, what they do, why they buy, etc and get in front of more people like them.

An example - take Saucony, let’s say their top customer segment is intermediate runners running their fifth or more marathons. Cool.

Knowing that they need to develop a social show targeting that individual with the right content.

This also means they can test different concepts and themes here. **They can test an entertaining concept. An educational concept. An inspirational concept.**

![[G-4HjqQXQAAPs__.png]]

But the key to this is how it’s positioned for the individual who’s an intermediate runner. This is determined by the theme of the show, then positioned properly by the script.

So, to understand your content buckets is to understand your customer base.

To quickly summarize, your content buckets determine who needs the social show / who you’re making it for.

## **Developing Your Content Pillars**

A **content pillar **is a core theme or topic area that a brand consistently creates content around. It serves as the foundation for all of your content ideas.

Meaning, it’s the large branches that build off the trunk of the tree, then all of your concepts and ideas that build off that pillar are the smaller branches.

Now, here’s where most people go wrong with their content pillars. They go to general. They say we want an educational pillar. An inspirational pillar. Sure. That doesn’t get you anywhere. You need to go layers deeper.

Back to Saucony.

![[G-4H2wEX0AEhKrc.jpeg]]

It needs to be a defined pillar so that you can truly brainstorm the best concepts underneath it.

So, now that pillar can look like…

An “Educational Pillar To Help Marathoners PR In Their Qualifying Race”

![[G-4H_uiWUAAfIUi.png]]

**So, here’s how to approach determining your content pillars:**

- **1-2 pillars MAX **If you have no winning content. Focus on testing more than anything.
- **2-3 content pillars** - If you have winning content. Anything that’s performed well, and performed well more than once.
- **3-5 pillars** - If you’re popping, know what works.

Then from there you ideate a show centered around these content pillars.

So, here’s what you’re going to do.

You’re going to imagine your narrative as the roots.

Your content buckets are the trunk.

Your branches are the content pillars.

And for this exercise, you’re going to determine two content pillars. Then attach a qualifier to each one of them by defining the value creation attached to each content pillar.

***Example:***

- **General:** Inspiration
- **Specific:** We’re going to inspire runners to push themselves past any limit they’ve ever set for themselves.
- **General:** Education
- **Specific:** Educational Pillar To Help Marathoners PR In Their Qualifying Race

Then, from here, you can truly ideate concepts and how to format those concepts based on the pillar.

Because true creativity lies within constraints.

## **Developing A Great Concept**

To develop a great concept, it needs to intersect between these elements:

- Your Narrative
- Association Factors
- Repeatable Concept
- Scalable Operations

![[G-4II_cXcAAeI5Q.png]]

Four elements where all great social shows live.

*Let’s look at an example…*

![[G-4JDN9WYAA6T6B.jpeg]]

**So, as you start to ideate, ask yourself these four questions:**

4. Does this concept amplify your narrative?
5. Does this associate my brand with aligned values, categories, or themes?
6. Is the concept repeatable?
7. Can we operationalize the logistics to be able to scale the concept?

If yes, the social show has legs.

## **The Repetitive Elements**

The goal of a social show is to build familiarity to the point that familiarity is the hook.

Someone should be able to watch the first frame and recognize what the content is.

And therefore, that first frame hooks them in.

Look at nearly any social show, and you’ll notice the repetitive elements. And that’s done strategically.

![[G-4JQmFWMAAH1BY.jpeg]]

**The hook. The location. The host. The theme. The format.**

All of the elements that make a show bingeable.

And also make a show memorable and familiar.

Which turns repetitive elements into your brand’s signature identity.

But before establishing them, you need to determine…

## **The Theme**

The theme is the backbone of your social show. It’s the central idea you use to shape the show’s content, context, creative direction, art direction, and ultimately how viewers will experience the show and who it will target.

**This means your theme:**

- Connects all episodes under one narrative
- Sets clear expectations for the viewer in the future
- Determines the style, mood, vibe

![[G-4JeIXXAAA3Nh_.jpeg]]

So, determining a theme comes from developing three aspects:

8. **The POV -** What specific stance, pov, or identity does your brand want to create?
9. **The Loop -** What makes people come back and/or binge the show? (different guests, different povs, etc)
10. **The Constraint - **What allows the show to be repeatable? (locations, guests, hosts, format)

Now, we hit the whiteboard for the ideation process.

## **How To Ideate Your Brand’s Social Show**

I’m keeping this extremely simple…

**You need to ask yourself six questions:**

11. Why does this show need to exist?
12. Does this show exist to amplify my brand’s narrative/throughline?
13. Where does this show take place and why?
14. What is the core action repeated episode after episode?
15. What makes the show bingeable?
16. What specific layer makes this unique to my brand?

From here, you can start packaging your social show.

## **How To Format Your Social Show**

How you format your show is how you package and deliver your show.

This means it determines the repeatable structure and rules that occur in every episode and ultimately, how every episode unfolds.

Matt Choi’s mile challenge is clearly a challenge, so every contestant sets a goal and is challenged to hit that goal. So, either people succeed or fail.

No different than Fear Factor back in the day.

A challenge. The challenge is then accepted by the participant. The participant either succeeds or fails.

And because social is replacing TV, the same formats that worked on TV are working on social.

![[G-4OITMXwAIM19l.jpeg]]

For you, this means taking your idea/concept and determining the format that best delivers your show in a way that your audience wants to consume AND allows new viewers to binge.

## **Determining Your Show’s Set**

Your show’s set is a format device.

Meaning, yes, the set is where it’s filmed, but more importantly determines why it’s filmed there.

Because your set will shape the behavior, structure, and repeatability of the show.

![[G-4ORasXwAAOTpM.jpeg]]

So, see, your set is your rulebook. And breaking the set = breaking the format.

To create your set, you need to run it through these 5 questions:

17. What environment do we want to put the viewer in?
18. What aspects, accessories, or props create recognition?
19. Does the set bring the concept and theme to life?
20. Can this set be repeated infinitely?
21. Will the set be instantly recognizable?

**And at the end of it all ask yourself:** *Does the set need to be explained*?

If yes. Back to the drawing board.

## **The Right Characters**

This is all about putting the right person in the right show because they will excel in the right format.

But the reality is, this is one of three people:

22. Someone you cast
23. Someone you work with (creators, influencers, ambassadors)
24. Someone who works with you (founder, employee)

And so the key is to understand your leverage points.

If you work with a founder-led brand, then leveraging your founder for a social show can be the easiest path to success.

If you work with no creators, influencers, and your founder doesn’t want to be behind the camera, but you have the budget, then you’re going to want to either cast actors and/or find creators to work with.

And sometimes the show is a combination of the above.

## **Key Element: Your Opening 3 Seconds**

Your show's hook needs to reveal itself instantly and be recognized after the first frame.

**This means starting every episode with the same:**

- Location
- Action
- Composition
- Verbal Cue and/or Visual Cue

![[G-4OiesWYAAOxP_.jpeg]]

This is the formula. The same sh*t. Scaled episode after episode. To form a clear value, visual, and verbal expectation that your opening 3 seconds meet every time.
