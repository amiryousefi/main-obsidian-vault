# **1. The Beginning**

I was born in a small village on the corner of Almaty, Kazakhstan.

A place where almost no one speaks English. Where the average salary is not more than $500/mo, and where people definitely haven't heard about Anthropic.

My mom is a teacher, and my dad is an entrepreneur. Since I was young, I have been passionate about entrepreneurship.

Hearing stories of Steve Jobs, Elon Musk, and Mark Zuckerberg inspired me and showed me that I don't have to be like everyone else.

My entrepreneurship journey goes straight into my childhood when I was reselling Hot Wheels cars for 3-5x the price, then I was charging my classmates to write exams, and so on. Everyone called me Elon Mark.

I've had a dream (and still do) to leave a big impact in my niche and do something massive to inspire a new generation of entrepreneurs.

# **2. Covid, English, and Medium**

Then the pandemic hit back in 2020.

When the whole world stopped, I just started.

The school shut down, and I've got a lot of free time that I could allocate in the way I wanted to.

I started by learning english. It was the first step to get access to the biggest market in the world. I spent the whole day sitting in Duolingo, watching videos on YouTube, reading books on grammar, and watching South Park with the subtitles.

It took me approximately 3-4 months to go from absolute zero to upper-intermediate in English.

While learning the basics, I also discovered [Medium.com](https://medium.com/). It was a great way to practice my reading in English and get a lot of knowledge on different topics.

After reading hundreds of aritcles, I randomly found an article that was telling how you can make money from Medium.

The thing that sets me apart from the most is the time between an idea and execution.

The same day I read this article, I wrote my first article. I think it was about self-improvement.

It didn't get a lot of views, but I started writing about all sorts of stuff. I was using Richard Feinman's technique - whenever you learn something, try to tell it to someone else.

I was consuming tons of books, YouTube videos, other Medium articles, and then just compressing them into my own articles.

On my 5th article i got 20k views and understood that I'm on the right path.

I was writing 3-4 articles every week for a few years straight. It led me to making $4-5k/mo just from monetization, top 200 best writers on the platform, millions of views on my articles. And all of that at the age of 15.

At the same time, I was learning programming (and writing articles about programming), and then randomly switched to design as it just seemed more appealing to me.

# **3. Then everything collapsed**

Then Medium shut down. The CEO changed, and they changed the algorithms.

Now it wasn't a part of the creator economy anymore. You could write an article that should get a lot of views, but it won't unless someone manually reviews it from their team and boosts it.

They wanted to have only high-quality and expert content and made a stake on that. As a result, all of my friends and I switched from Medium to X.

***Ps. It was the best decision of my life.***

At first, I didn't know how to get followers on X. It was a new platform, with new algorithms, and with a different audience.

It took a couple of months to get momentum and see that it's working.

But after spending more than 2 years on this platform - here's the exact template I would use if I started from scratch on X:

**0-500 followers:** become a 'reply guy'. Leave 30-50 comments daily under the accounts you're interested in and your ICP. Create a list of these accounts on X, and engage with them daily. You shouldn't just leave comments like "Amazing", "Congrats", etc.; it should be something meaningful (your thoughts, opinion, feedback, or a question). Comment more than posting, you should still have a few high-quality posts on your profile, but it doesn't matter, as no one would read them for now.

**500-5k:** start doing giveaways/lead magnets. You can give away 3 things: *product*, *service*, or *knowledge*. I was giving away some Figma/Framer templates. You can give away different guides, digital products, or provide a free consultation. Here's an example of a giveaway:

# **4. How I got my first clients**

But finding clients was a nightmare.

I had 5-6k followers and didn't get a single lead. So, seeing people with 200 followers and zero design posts complaining about clients makes me laugh.

I've tried everything, but could only get a couple of leads that paid me **$1200 for a landing.**

Abu was handling the whole production and management.

A single launch video takes 3-4 weeks for us to produce, so giving it away for free means a pretty significant loss for the business. But we're willing to take this risk, and always will.

If you're starting out, I'd advise you to consistently become better and better and **never "value yourself".**

Valuing yourself gives you an ego that you should completely remove in the beginning. You should be willing to work for free for months, send thousands of cold DMs without getting a response, hear the harsh feedback, and become better with every iteration.

Thinking that people will value you just because you exist is dumb. Even dumber to decide to charge **$20k** for a landing page when you don't have a name.

It's definitely not easy, but with a lot of volume, it pays off. Volume kills luck.

Now our workflow is pretty straightforward. I just squeeze as many posts out of every case study and generate content on a daily basis that generates us a consistent lead flow.

I'm still attending all the calls myself cuz I just love exploring more businesses and meeting new people.

I became friends with most of my clients, and we travel together, having lunches together, and just chatting outside of work.
