## What's the Pomodoro Technique?

The technique was ‘invented’ in the early 1990s by a developer, entrepreneur, and author Francesco Cirillo.

Cirillo noticed that when faced with large tasks or a series of assignments, we’re able to be most effective if we break the work down into short, timed intervals (called “Pomodoros”) which are spaced out by short breaks.

**In essence, it is a cyclical system based around working in short bursts of 25 minutes with intermittent breaks, of 5 minutes, which can help to improve motivation, creativity and efficiency.**

## How I Use It

I used to use the Pomodoro Technique back when I was a student as a way to motivate myself to long study sessions. My problem with this technique is that I personally warm up after 20-30 minutes of doing work so in a classic version of the Pomodoro Technique that's exactly when I'd have to stop working. If you're having the same issue a good thing to do is to switch the 25/5 minutes intervals to something like 45/15 minutes where you do the work for 45 minutes and after that, you take a 15-minute break.
