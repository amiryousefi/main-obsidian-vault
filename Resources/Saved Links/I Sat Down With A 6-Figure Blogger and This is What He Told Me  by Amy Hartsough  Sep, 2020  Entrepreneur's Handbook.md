## “Focus on Impacting 1 Person, Not 1,000,000” — Tom Kuegler

Photo by [Glenn Carstens-Peters](https://unsplash.com/@glenncarstenspeters?utm_source=medium&utm_medium=referral) on [Unsplash](https://unsplash.com/?utm_source=medium&utm_medium=referral)

I first met [Tom Kuegler](https://medium.com/@tomkuegler) when I took one of his courses about how to make money online as a writer. The more I got to learn about his journey, however, the more I wanted to learn how he’s been able to successfully monetize his content across various platforms.

For those of you who don’t know Tom, he’s a writer, Youtuber, Facebook Influencer, and a course creator for platforms like Medium and Linkedin who has amassed a following of 500,000 people.

All in, according to Tom, on average he’s making over $8,000 a month from writing, the ad revenue from Facebook and Youtube, and selling his online courses which makes up a high percentage of his income.

Tom and I spent a good hour talking about the lessons he has learned and what advice he has for people like me who are just starting out.

Below are the tips that stood out the most.

# Create “Magic” For Those Who Do Business With You

He shared a story with me about what drives him to help people with his content and course offerings. Towards the end of college, he worked as an intern at Disney World for eight months. His experience changed the way he looks at business. Here’s Tom in his own words:

> “I worked at Disney World and we were challenged every day to make ‘magical moments’ for guests. Sometimes we’d give away free cupcakes and sometimes we’d sit on the pavement with guests and draw Disney characters in chalk. I want to create magical moments for those who take my course and take time to learn from me in that same regard.”

That story really encapsulates Tom’s approach to business. As he learned at Disney, generosity seems to be at the forefront of his decision-making. Here are some other valuable business lessons Tom imparted to me. Imagine that everything below is in quotes.

# **Build An Email List to 1,000 People**

Build an audience first. Work like hell to build a substantial audience. This takes at least 1 year. While you build your audience, build an email list. If you fail to build an email list then most of your effort growing on a platform was wasted. I’d say you need to work for a year until you’re ready to monetize an online course.

# **Focus on Impacting 1 Person, Not 1,000,000**

I want to move people with a message. I want to give people some of the lessons I learned so they don’t have to go through the trouble of learning those lessons themselves. That’s all my writing is — just me telling people what I’ve learned in my life. I don’t assume it will help a million people, but it will most likely help one person. If I’ve helped one person, that’s enough for me. Sometimes I get so annoyed with folks who quit after getting 10 views on a blog post. That’s 10 people who read your work. That’s incredible. 10 strangers read your words. We have devalued the value of 1 person far too much in this industry. That’s what I stand for.

# Constantly Try Things Way Outside Your Comfort Zone

I’m working on two online courses and I’m also working on building two WordPress sites outside of my blog. Both online courses are extremely out of the ordinary for me. One is on goal-setting and productivity and the other is basically a collection of advice I’m gathering from 15 experts.

I’m the creativity guy, not the productivity guy. For a company to hear my pitch and decide to fund my idea was incredible to me and I feel blessed. I think it’s important to just say “screw it” every now and then and try something way outside your comfort zone. I landed that course deal after spending 5 hours ideating the course. I plotted out the lessons, what I’d teach in those lessons, and how those lessons worked for me. I wrote a 2-page summary of the course and sent it over in a pitch to the company I wanted to work with. It could’ve been a complete waste of time but I had a funny feeling they might accept it.

Lo and behold, it worked. The blogging guy is making a course on goal setting and productivity. Who knew?

I think that testing the limits of your brand is so important, too. Outside of my regular blog, I am known as the “Philippines” vlogger. I vlog about the Philippines. I have two separate audiences who want two separate types of content from me. I think you need to constantly test out new topics as a creator. You never know what could happen.

# **When Hiring, Wait to See Who Reaches Out to You**

I have an Editor for my publication on one platform and an Editor for my videos on Facebook and Youtube. My advice is simple...

Wait to see who reaches out to you. Reward the folks who send a personal message to you saying “I like your work, especially X and Y, and I’d love to work with you someday if you ever have an opportunity open.” Or at least give them the opportunity first.

I love people like that. They’re hustlers. They’re real. They’re go-getters. They create their own opportunities. When you get big enough online to actually need help, then you’ll start getting messages like these. I also want to say that for folks who are thinking of hiring help, I think it’s best to hold off on hiring help for as long as possible. Gain the skills you want to hire somebody for. That will help with your own personal growth. The only reason you should hire help in my mind is because you literally do not have enough time in the day.

# **Diversify Your Revenue Streams**

2017 was the start of my entrepreneurial journey. I made maybe $30,000 that year freelancing and selling my course at the VERY end of the year.

All I had for a while was my online course. I started a vlog on Youtube and Facebook in 2018 that didn’t really monetize all that well until the very end of the year. Then I made $6,000 from one video on Facebook after it went viral and got 6,000,000 views. That was December of 2018. Seeing the ad revenue from that was bonkers. It was like the value of a used car just materialized right in front of my eyes on my computer. It just all seemed too easy.

The real answer is that I’ve diversified my revenue streams over the last few years. I’m always working on a new way to monetize. Once I get that setup, then I make more money. When I monetized my Facebook page, I made more money per month immediately. Another element to this equation is getting people into my funnel. The more folks I can get into my funnel, the more money I make. My blog is a great way to fill the funnel, but I’ve since created a WordPress website that averages about 20,000 views per month that gets me a lot of leads, too. I also grew a bunch on LinkedIn and use my audience there to fill my funnel.

# **Do Two Things, Always**

I’m doing two things, always... I’m growing an audience somewhere and creating another product. That’s the two things you need to do to make money online.
