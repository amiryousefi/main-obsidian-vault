# Amir 2.0: A Personal Transformation Instruction Book

**Prepared for:** Amir, owner of **your business**  
**Purpose:** To move from **Amir 1.0**, the ambitious but stuck operator, into **Amir 2.0**, the clear, disciplined, healthy, strategic builder.  
**Method:** Honest assessment, created direction, and practical systems. No motivational theater. No vague self-help. No pretending.

## How to Use This Book

This book is not meant to be read like entertainment. It is meant to be worked through like a training manual. If you only read it, nothing changes. If you do the exercises, write the answers, make the decisions, and keep the commitments, the plan becomes real.

The transformation has three phases. **Phase 1** shows Amir exactly where he is now. **Phase 2** helps Amir get unstuck, choose direction, and design the future self. **Phase 3** turns that direction into daily behavior, business discipline, health routines, relationship standards, and a practical operating system.

The tone of this book is intentionally direct. Amir does not need more comforting lies. He also does not need shame. He needs accurate information, strong standards, and repeatable action. The point is not to attack Amir 1.0. The point is to stop letting Amir 1.0 run the whole business, body, mind, calendar, and future.

| Transformation Phase | Core Question | Main Output |
|---|---|---|
| **Phase 1: Know Where You Are** | What is the honest current state of Amir 1.0? | A written audit of life, business, patterns, excuses, strengths, and constraints. |
| **Phase 2: Get Unstuck & Find Direction** | What does Amir 2.0 actually want, and what future is worth becoming disciplined for? | A created future, identity statement, 12-month direction, and one clear bridge plan. |
| **Phase 3: The Practical Plan** | What must Amir do daily, weekly, and monthly to become that person? | Habits, systems, routines, environment changes, relationship standards, and setback protocols. |

> **Rule of the book:** If an answer stays in your head, it does not count. Write it down. The Ali Abdaal resources repeatedly frame life direction as a navigation problem: you need to know where you are, where you want to go, and what path connects the two.[^ali-growth] Matt D’Avella’s Unstuck Challenge makes the same point in a simpler way: you do not get unstuck by consuming more content; you get unstuck by asking better questions, making decisions, and practicing one behavior consistently.[^unstuck]

# Phase 1: Know Where You Are — Amir 1.0

## The Purpose of Phase 1

Phase 1 is not about judging yourself. It is about locating yourself. A man who is lost does not need to scream at himself for being lost. He needs coordinates. Right now, Amir may have ambition, ideas, taste, and a real-world business in your business, but ambition without accurate self-knowledge becomes noise. It produces late-night thinking, scattered plans, emotional spikes, and then another week that looks too much like the last one.

The first phase is designed to make **Amir 1.0 visible**. You will define what is working, what is not working, what patterns keep repeating, what hidden payoffs keep you stuck, which life areas are being neglected, which excuses are being protected, and which strengths are already available. This is not a confession booth. It is an operating-room diagnosis.

Dan Koe’s Human 3.0 framework warns against solving life through only one lens. A business problem is not always solved by more motivation. A discipline problem is not always solved by a better calendar. A confidence problem may involve body, mind, social environment, and competence at the same time.[^human30] The Pileh course makes a related point through the idea of a **default world**: you do not respond only to facts; you respond to your interpretations, old judgments, language, and unconscious patterns.[^pileh-en] Phase 1 exists so those patterns stop managing you from the dark.

## The Amir 1.0 Baseline

Before changing anything, name the current version honestly. Amir 1.0 is not a failure. Amir 1.0 built enough ambition, taste, and courage to run a business. That matters. your business is not an imaginary dream in a notebook; it is a real environment with customers, operations, staff dynamics, costs, branding, community potential, and daily pressure. But Amir 1.0 also has patterns that are too expensive to keep.

Amir 1.0 may be the version who wants the business to grow but spends too much time reacting. He may want stronger health but lets stress, sleep, and convenience decide. He may want deeper relationships but waits for connection to happen accidentally. He may want strategic growth but keeps getting pulled into operational fires. He may want discipline but negotiates with himself every morning. He may want freedom but keeps repeating the routines that create the same ceiling.

This is the first hard truth: **your current life is not random**. It is being produced by your current standards, environment, language, habits, relationships, calendar, and hidden rewards. If you want Amir 2.0, stop asking only, “Why am I stuck?” Start asking, “What system is perfectly designed to keep producing Amir 1.0?”

## Exercise 1: The No-Bullshit Current-State Audit

Set a timer for 45 minutes. Open a notebook or a document titled **Amir 1.0 Audit**. Do not make it beautiful. Do not optimize the format. Write fast and tell the truth. This exercise draws from the Unstuck Challenge Day 1 prompts, Ali Abdaal’s work and life audits, and the Think Day method.[^unstuck][^ali-growth][^think-day]

| Area | What Is Working? | What Is Not Working? | Cost of Ignoring It for 12 More Months |
|---|---|---|---|
| **your business / Career** | What is already strong about the business, your skills, your ideas, your customer understanding, or your reputation? | Where are you reactive, inconsistent, unclear, financially sloppy, operationally overloaded, or under-strategic? | What happens if the business remains at the same level one year from now? |
| **Health / Energy** | What habits, strengths, or physical advantages do you still have? | Where are sleep, movement, food, stress, body composition, or energy below standard? | What does another year of low energy cost your confidence and business? |
| **Relationships / Family / Friends** | Who supports you? Where are you already loyal, caring, or reliable? | Where are you isolated, unavailable, distracted, resentful, or passive? | What relationships weaken if you keep waiting? |
| **Purpose / Direction** | What still excites you? What do you feel proud of building? | Where are you vague, borrowed, comparison-driven, or unclear? | What does another year of vague ambition create? |
| **Habits / Discipline** | Which promises do you already keep? Where do you show up? | Where do you start and stop? Where do you negotiate? | What does repeated self-betrayal do to self-trust? |
| **Money / Systems** | What income, assets, knowledge, or opportunities exist now? | Where are finances unclear, untracked, emotionally driven, or dependent on one stream? | What future options disappear if this remains vague? |
| **Joy / Adventure / Play** | What still gives you aliveness? | Where has life become repetitive, numb, or only duty? | What happens if you win externally but feel dead internally? |

After completing the table, write one paragraph beginning with this sentence: **“If I am brutally honest, Amir 1.0 is being kept in place by…”** Do not soften the answer. Do not dramatize it either. Accuracy is the standard.

Two questions matter more than the score itself. First, ask **why the score is not lower**. Small actions are how identity becomes believable.

## Exercise 3: The Human 3.0 Quadrant Map

Dan Koe’s Human 3.0 model divides development into **Mind, Body, Spirit, and Vocation**. This is useful because stuck people often try to solve every problem with their favorite tool. If Amir likes business strategy, he may try to solve emotional avoidance with another business plan. If Amir likes mindset content, he may try to solve poor sleep with better affirmations. That is partial thinking. The quadrant map forces precision.[^human30]

| Quadrant | What It Covers | Amir 1.0 Diagnostic Questions | Current Bottleneck |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Mind** | Thoughts, beliefs, attention, emotions, interpretations, self-talk. | What story do I keep telling about myself? What fear is managing my attention? What thought pattern returns when I am about to act? |  |
| **Body** | Sleep, food, training, grooming, posture, energy, nervous system, physical confidence. | Is my body helping my ambition or taxing it? What physical habit is quietly lowering my standards? |  |
| **Spirit** | Meaning, relationships, family, values, community, contribution, connection. | Who am I becoming for the people around me? Where am I disconnected from meaning or people? |  |
| **Vocation** | your business, money, skills, career, systems, service, business, contribution. | What problem am I solving, for whom, and how well? Where is the business dependent on chaos or personality instead of systems? |  |

Write one sentence for each quadrant using this format: **“In the area of [quadrant], Amir 1.0 is currently…”** Then write a second sentence: **“The next trait Amir 2.0 must build here is…”**

This is not a personality test. It is a development map. The goal is to identify the next trait, not to create a permanent label. If the Mind bottleneck is avoidance, the trait may be emotional honesty. If the Body bottleneck is low energy, the trait may be physical consistency. If the Spirit bottleneck is isolation, the trait may be initiation. If the Vocation bottleneck is reactive operation, the trait may be strategic leadership.

## Exercise 4: Identify Your Rackets

The Pileh course uses the word **racket** for a persistent complaint or unwanted pattern that secretly gives you a payoff. The payoff might be being right, avoiding responsibility, receiving sympathy, staying safe, feeling superior, avoiding risk, or not having to make a decision. The cost is usually intimacy, joy, freedom, power, and self-expression.[^pileh-en]

This concept is uncomfortable because it removes the luxury of pure victimhood. It does not deny that circumstances can be hard. It asks a sharper question: **What do I get from keeping this complaint alive?**

| Complaint or Pattern | Hidden Payoff | Real Cost | Amir 2.0 Replacement |
|---|---|---|---|
| “I do not have enough time.” | Avoids choosing, prioritizing, or disappointing people. | The business and body grow only when life leaves spare time, which it rarely does. | “I create protected time for what matters.” |
| “I am not consistent.” | Explains failure before trying and lowers expectations. | Self-trust keeps shrinking. | “I keep small promises until consistency becomes normal.” |
| “The market is difficult.” | Makes business stagnation feel externally caused. | Strategy, offers, customer insight, and execution do not improve. | “I study the market, serve better, and adapt faster.” |
| “I need the perfect plan first.” | Protects against criticism and imperfect action. | No real feedback arrives because nothing ships. | “I test, learn, and iterate in public.” |
| “People do not understand my vision.” | Protects ego and avoids clearer communication. | The vision remains lonely and unenrolled. | “I speak clearly, invite people in, and build evidence.” |

Now write your own. Complete at least five rackets. For each one, be honest about the hidden reward. If you cannot find the payoff, you are probably still defending the racket.

The goal is not to shame yourself for having rackets. Everyone has them. The goal is to stop paying for them with your future. Amir 2.0 does not need to be right about why things are hard. Amir 2.0 needs to become powerful inside reality as it is.

## Exercise 5: Integrity Audit — Where Your Word Is Leaking Power

Pileh defines **integrity** as honoring your word. This includes explicit promises, things you say you will do, expectations you allow others to have, standards you know are required, and responsibilities attached to the roles you occupy. Integrity is not moral decoration. It is operational power. When your word is weak, your life fragments.[^pileh-en]

For Amir, integrity directly affects your business. A business depends on trust: customer experience, staff expectations, cleanliness, financial clarity, event promises, opening hours, equipment quality, community standards, and brand reputation. If Amir’s word is loose in private, it will eventually show up in the business.

| Area of Word | Promises Made | Promises Kept? | If Not, What Must Be Cleaned Up? |
|---|---|---|---|
| Promises to yourself | Health, wake time, planning, learning, deep work, money tracking. |  |  |
| Promises to your business customers | Experience, quality, events, atmosphere, service, consistency. |  |  |
| Promises to staff or partners | Communication, pay, expectations, feedback, decisions. |  |  |
| Promises to family or close relationships | Presence, time, respect, support, reliability. |  |  |
| Promises implied by your ambition | If you say you want growth, what does growth require from you? |  |  |

For every broken promise, choose one of three actions. **Fulfill it**, if it still matters. **Renegotiate it**, if it cannot or should not be fulfilled as originally stated. **Clean it up**, if someone was affected by your lack of integrity. Cleaning it up means acknowledging it directly, without hiding behind reasons, justifications, or explanations.

This is where many ambitious people lose power. They collect goals, declarations, and visions, but do not repair the places where their word has already become cheap. Amir 2.0 is built on a stronger relationship with his own word.

## Exercise 6: The Mind Game Audit

Dan Koe’s “Life Is a Mind Game” argues that the mind protects the current identity. When you try to change, the old self treats the new self as a threat. That threat can appear as anxiety, distraction, rationalization, tiredness, cynicism, sudden research, sudden hunger, or the urge to reorganize everything except the one task that matters.[^mind-game]

This is not an excuse. It is an instruction. Expect resistance. Study it. Catch it earlier.

| Trigger Moment | Old-Self Response | What It Protects | Amir 2.0 Response |
|---|---|---|---|
| Before deep work on your business growth | Check phone, handle small tasks, browse ideas. | Protects from judgment, uncertainty, and hard thinking. | Start a 25-minute focused block with one defined output. |
| Before exercise | Negotiate, delay, promise tomorrow. | Protects comfort and low-energy identity. | Put on shoes and complete the minimum version. |
| When numbers or finances feel unclear | Avoid the spreadsheet. | Protects from feeling behind or exposed. | Open the numbers for 10 minutes; clarity before emotion. |
| When lonely or disconnected | Wait for others to initiate. | Protects from rejection. | Send one invitation or message. |
| When a plan is imperfect | Research more. | Protects from public failure. | Ship version 1 and collect feedback. |

Train the **gap between impulse and response**. When the old impulse appears, do nothing for ten seconds. Do not obey it. Do not argue with it. Watch it. Then choose the smallest Amir 2.0 action available. The goal is not perfect discipline. The goal is noticing the old identity sooner and feeding it less often.

# Phase 2: Get Unstuck & Find Direction — The Bridge

## The Purpose of Phase 2

Phase 1 told the truth about Amir 1.0. Phase 2 creates the bridge to Amir 2.0. This phase is not about finding a perfect life purpose in one dramatic moment. That is another trap. Direction is not discovered only by thinking. Direction is produced through written vision, honest desire, tested possibilities, useful constraints, and action.

Ali Abdaal frames feeling lost as a navigation problem. You need a current location, a destination, and a path.[^ali-growth] Matt D’Avella’s Unstuck Challenge moves from dissatisfaction into visualization, forgiveness, focus, and one habit.[^unstuck] Pileh adds a sharper distinction: you can either live from a **default future**, which is the past repeating itself with new dates, or you can create a **created future**, which is declared and then acted into existence.[^pileh-en] Dan Koe adds the missing intensity: if your future has no emotional gravity, your old identity will keep winning.[^fix-life][^mind-game]

For Amir, the bridge must answer a practical question: **What future is worth becoming disciplined for?** Not what future sounds impressive. Not what future would look good online. Not what future would satisfy someone else’s expectations. What future is strong enough that you will change your sleep, calendar, body, standards, business systems, friendships, and attention to make it real?

## Stop Waiting for Perfect Clarity

Analysis paralysis often disguises itself as intelligence. You research. You compare paths. You imagine possible outcomes. You plan the plan. You watch one more video. You tell yourself you are being strategic. Sometimes that is true. Often, you are avoiding the humiliation of imperfect action.

The solution is not reckless action. The solution is **structured exploration**. Ali Abdaal’s business-direction resource uses the creative process of **diverge, converge, emerge**: generate many possibilities, narrow them intelligently, then let a clearer path emerge through testing.[^business-2026] This is the correct antidote to perfectionism. You do not demand certainty before movement. You create enough clarity to take the next intelligent step.

| Stuck Pattern | What Amir 1.0 Does | What Amir 2.0 Does |
|---|---|---|
| **Analysis paralysis** | Keeps thinking until the decision feels safe. | Sets a decision deadline and runs an experiment. |
| **Perfectionism** | Waits until the plan, brand, body, offer, or routine is flawless. | Ships the first useful version and improves from feedback. |
| **Borrowed ambition** | Wants what looks impressive to others. | Chooses based on energy, service, values, and lived evidence. |
| **Fear of judgment** | Keeps ideas private to avoid being seen failing. | Builds evidence publicly and lets reality train him. |
| **Too many goals** | Tries to change body, business, money, friendships, and mindset at once. | Chooses one leverage priority for the next 30 days. |

The bridge is built by making decisions that are **specific, reversible, and reviewable**. Most choices are not cliffs. They are experiments. Treat your business growth, health routines, content, community events, partnerships, and personal habits as experiments with feedback loops. You are not trying to prove your genius. You are trying to learn faster than Amir 1.0 avoids.

Write the answer as a full page. Do not summarize it in three clever sentences. Describe a normal Tuesday five years from now if Amir 1.0 remains in charge.

This sentence should bother you. If it does not, it is too polite. The purpose is not to hate yourself. The purpose is to stop treating the current pattern as harmless. Every repeated week is a vote. If the same week continues for five years, it becomes a life.

## Exercise 2: Release the Old Story

The Unstuck Challenge Day 3 uses forgiveness and reframing to release past baggage.[^unstuck] Pileh uses the idea of **incompletions**, which are unresolved past issues that occupy the space of the future.[^pileh-en] The shared insight is simple: if you carry old failure as identity, you will drag it into every new attempt.

Write a letter to yourself beginning with:

> **Amir, I forgive you for the ways you started and stopped. I forgive you for the times you confused inconsistency with identity. I forgive you for the moments when you knew what needed to be done and still avoided it. I am not using the past as proof that the future is closed. I am using it as data.**

Then complete this table. Do not make the new belief fake. It must be constructive and believable.

| Old Story | What It Protected | New Belief | Small Proof This Week |
|---|---|---|---|
| “I am not disciplined.” | Protected you from expecting more of yourself. | “I become disciplined by keeping small promises daily.” |  |
| “I need more clarity first.” | Protected you from visible mistakes. | “Clarity increases through action and feedback.” |  |
| “I do not have enough time.” | Protected you from choosing and cutting. | “I create time by deciding what matters less.” |  |
| “your business cannot grow until conditions improve.” | Protected you from strategic responsibility. | “I can improve offers, systems, community, and execution inside imperfect conditions.” |  |
| “I have failed before.” | Protected you from trying with full commitment. | “Failure is information; repetition with adjustment is training.” |  |

This is not therapy language for comfort. It is operational language for movement. The old story must be replaced by a story that produces action.

Ali Abdaal discusses **mimetic desire**, the tendency to want things because other people want them or because culture rewards them.[^ali-growth] This is dangerous for Amir because the gaming, entrepreneurship, and self-improvement worlds are full of seductive images: flashy business growth, luxury, founder status, viral content, passive income, extreme discipline, and public success. Amir 2.0 does not chase borrowed symbols. He chooses the life whose **process** he is willing to repeat.

## Exercise 7: The Created Future Declaration

Pileh’s created future is not a prediction. It is a declaration that creates a new world of action.[^pileh-en] A weak future says, “I hope things get better.” A created future says, “This is the future I am standing for, and my actions now will be organized around it.”

Write a declaration using this structure:

> **I declare that by [date], Amir 2.0 is [identity], your business is [business/community promise], my body is [health standard], my relationships are [connection standard], and my daily life is organized around [core principles]. I create a future where people associate your business with belonging, competition, quality, and memorable experiences. I do not wait to feel ready. Now make the declaration practical. Pileh emphasizes constructive language: **promises, declarations, invitations, requests, and commands**.[^pileh-en] These are not motivational words. They are tools for moving reality.

The created future must produce behavior now. If the declaration does not change tomorrow’s calendar, it is decoration.

# Phase 3: The Practical Plan — Becoming Amir 2.0

## The Purpose of Phase 3

Phase 3 turns the created future into behavior. This is where most ambitious people fail. They do the audit, feel the pain, write the vision, get inspired, and then try to become a completely different person by Monday morning. That is not transformation. That is emotional overcorrection.

Amir 2.0 is built through **systems, habits, standards, environment, relationships, and review loops**. Ali Abdaal’s systems resource makes the point directly: goals describe what you want, but systems define the repeatable process that produces it.[^systems] The discipline-era resource reframes discipline as self-respect in action, not punishment.[^discipline] The ambitious-but-lazy resource identifies the real gap: ambitious people often have vision but lack protected time and consistent action.[^ambitious-lazy] Matt D’Avella’s Unstuck Challenge ends with one 30-day habit because consistency beats intensity.[^unstuck]

The practical plan is built around one principle:

> **Amir 2.0 does not rely on mood. He lives inside systems that make the right action easier, the wrong action harder, and self-deception more visible.**

## The Amir 2.0 Operating Identity

Before habits, define the identity that the habits serve. Dan Koe’s reset framework argues that behavior follows identity, goals, and worldview.[^fix-life] If you try to install Amir 2.0 habits on Amir 1.0 identity, the old self will sabotage the new routine. The identity must become clear enough that daily actions feel like evidence.

Write this identity statement and refine it until it feels direct, serious, and believable:

> **I am Amir 2.0: a disciplined builder, strategic owner, healthy man, reliable leader, and creator of a stronger gaming community through your business. I keep my word. I protect my time. I train my body. I lead the business with systems instead of chaos. I build relationships intentionally. I do not confuse thinking with progress. I act, review, and improve.**

This statement is not magic. It is a standard. Every system below exists to make this identity harder to forget.

## Choose the First 30-Day Focus

The Unstuck Challenge warns that trying to improve everything at once creates burnout and quitting.[^unstuck] For the first 30 days, Amir must choose one leverage focus. This does not mean ignoring everything else. It means one area gets the main transformation energy while other areas receive minimum standards.

| Candidate Focus | When to Choose It | One-Year Goal Example | 30-Day Habit Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| **your business Growth** | Choose this if business stagnation is the main bottleneck and you need strategic momentum. | Build your business into a more systemized, event-driven, community-centered business. | Complete one 60-minute strategic growth block every operating day. |
| **Health / Energy** | Choose this if low energy, body dissatisfaction, sleep, or stress is weakening everything else. | Become visibly stronger, leaner, more energetic, and more confident. | Train or walk for 30 minutes daily, with a 10-minute minimum. |
| **Discipline / Self-Trust** | Choose this if the biggest pain is repeatedly breaking promises to yourself. | Become a man whose word to himself is reliable. | Complete the morning anchor routine every day for 30 days. |
| **Money / Financial Clarity** | Choose this if unclear numbers are creating anxiety or limiting business decisions. | Know personal and business finances clearly and build a path toward resilience. | Review numbers or take one money action for 15 minutes daily. |
| **Relationships / Community** | Choose this if isolation, weak friendships, or passive connection is costing joy and support. | Build a stronger personal circle and a stronger your business community. | Send one thoughtful message, invitation, or follow-up daily. |

For most versions of Amir, the strongest first focus is probably **Discipline / Self-Trust** or **Health / Energy**, because every other goal depends on execution capacity. If your energy is poor and your word to yourself is weak, business strategy becomes fragile. However, if your business is currently at a critical point, then business growth may need the first 30-day sprint. Choose based on the highest leverage, not the most exciting fantasy.

Write your commitment in this format:

> **For the next 30 days, I will focus on [one area] because it creates the highest leverage for Amir 2.0 right now. My one-year goal in this area is [specific outcome]. My daily habit is [specific behavior]. My minimum version is [tiny version]. I am practicing this because I am becoming the kind of man who [identity].**

## Habits to Build

The goal is not to collect habits. The goal is to install the few habits that make Amir 2.0 inevitable. A habit earns its place only if it supports energy, clarity, business growth, relationships, or self-trust.

| Habit to Build | Standard Version | Minimum Version | Why It Matters | Source Insight |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Morning anchor** | Wake, water, no phone, 5 minutes silence, plan top three, begin first important action. | Water, no phone for 10 minutes, write one priority. | Starts the day with self-command instead of reaction. | Discipline as self-respect; systems over motivation.[^discipline][^systems] |
| **Daily movement** | Strength training, cardio, walk, or mobility for 30–60 minutes. | 10-minute walk or mobility. | Builds energy, mood, confidence, and self-trust. | Health as foundation; non-negotiable minimums.[^systems][^discipline] |
| **your business growth block** | 60–90 minutes working on strategy, offers, systems, marketing, events, finance, or partnerships. | 20 minutes on one defined output. | Moves from operator mode to owner mode. | Protected time; value creation.[^ambitious-lazy][^business-2026] |
| **Daily shutdown** | Review day, clean workspace, choose tomorrow’s first task, set phone away. | Write tomorrow’s first task. | Reduces morning decision fatigue and prevents drift. | Lower activation energy; pre-decide behavior.[^discipline] |
| **Weekly review** | Review goals, calendar, finances, habits, lessons, and next week’s plan. | 20-minute review of wins, problems, and next actions. | Creates feedback loop and prevents silent drifting. | Cybernetic iteration; weekly systems.[^fix-life][^systems] |
| **Relationship initiation** | Two to four intentional touchpoints weekly. | One message or invitation weekly. | Builds support and connection instead of accidental isolation. | Relationship systems; service and contribution.[^systems][^ali-growth] |
| **Learning / skill block** | 30–60 minutes on business, leadership, marketing, finance, health, or communication. | 10 minutes reading or notes. | Builds the traits Amir 2.0 needs instead of imitating the image. | Human 3.0 traits; false transformation warning.[^human30] |

A minimum version is not a loophole. It is a continuity device. The minimum habit exists so one bad day does not become a broken identity. If you cannot train for 45 minutes, walk for 10. If you cannot complete a full growth block, define and complete one small business action. If you cannot do the perfect morning, keep one promise. Never let the old self use imperfection as a reason to disappear.

## Habits to Break

Do not try to break a habit by simply hating it. Replace the function it serves. Most bad habits solve a short-term emotional problem. Scrolling may relieve uncertainty. Overplanning may protect from judgment. Late nights may create fake freedom after a stressful day. Avoidance may protect from seeing numbers or feedback. If you remove the behavior without addressing the function, it returns.

| Habit to Break | What It Is Really Doing | Replacement Standard | Environmental Change |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Phone first thing in the morning** | Lets outside inputs own your attention before you choose your life. | No phone until after morning anchor and first priority. | Charge phone outside the bedroom or across the room. |
| **Reactive business work all day** | Feels productive while avoiding strategic ownership. | First growth block before low-value admin when possible. | Create a visible weekly owner-mode schedule. |
| **Overplanning without shipping** | Protects from feedback and imperfection. | Every plan must produce a shipped test, request, offer, or conversation. | Use a decision deadline and public accountability. |
| **Late-night drift** | Provides short-term escape and destroys next-day energy. | Fixed shutdown and sleep window. | Remove screens from bed; set evening alarm. |
| **Avoiding numbers** | Protects from discomfort and uncertainty. | Weekly money review for personal and business finances. | Keep finance dashboard simple and visible. |
| **Waiting for others to initiate** | Protects from rejection. | Initiate connection weekly. | Add recurring relationship reminders. |
| **Consuming self-improvement instead of acting** | Creates the feeling of progress without risk. | One hour of action before any growth content. | No educational content until daily priority is done. |

Dan Koe’s mind-game resource says the old self is partly stored in the environment.[^mind-game] This means your phone, room, desk, feeds, default apps, business routines, friend patterns, and late-night habits are not neutral. They are memory systems for Amir 1.0. If you leave them untouched, do not be surprised when they reinstall the old identity.

## The your business Growth System

your business is not only Amir’s business. It is one of Amir’s training grounds. The business can either keep Amir trapped in reactive operations or force him to become a strategic leader. The difference is whether Amir installs systems.

Ali Abdaal’s business framework says money is an exchange of value and that a strong opportunity starts with a specific person, a painful problem, and a useful solution.[^business-2026] For your business, this means growth should not depend on vague hopes like “more customers.” It should depend on better offers, better community, better events, better retention, better partnerships, and clearer numbers.

| Weekly Business System | Output | Why It Matters |
|---|---|---|
| **Customer insight loop** | Speak with or collect feedback from 5–10 customers weekly. | Stops guessing and reveals real reasons people return or disappear. |
| **Event engine** | Plan, promote, run, and review one recurring event format. | Turns your business into a community habit, not just a venue. |
| **Retention system** | Track repeat customers, memberships, loyalty, or community participation. | Growth is easier when people come back. |
| **Content / visibility system** | Post highlights, winners, setups, community moments, and offers consistently. | Makes the business visible and gives people a reason to talk. |
| **Partnership pipeline** | Contact local schools, creators, brands, teams, or communities. | Expands reach beyond walk-ins and existing circles. |
| **Finance review** | Review revenue, costs, margins, cash, best days, weak days, and experiments. | Reduces anxiety and improves decisions. |
| **Operations cleanup** | Identify one repeated friction and create a checklist, rule, or delegation. | Frees Amir from solving the same problem repeatedly. |

Each week, choose one **business boss fight**. A boss fight is the main business problem of the week, borrowed from Dan Koe’s “life as a video game” framing.[^fix-life] Examples include filling a tournament, creating a membership offer, fixing a recurring customer complaint, improving the booking process, training staff on one standard, or negotiating a partnership. The boss fight must have a visible result by the end of the week.

| Weekly Boss Fight | Success Definition | First Action | Review Result |
|---|---|---|---|
|  |  |  |  |

Do not call admin work a boss fight unless it genuinely moves the business. Updating a spreadsheet may matter. Rearranging notes for the tenth time probably does not. Amir 2.0 knows the difference between motion and progress.

## The Health and Energy System

Health is not a side quest. It is the energy infrastructure for everything else. The systems resource uses the quote, “The healthy man has a thousand wishes, the sick man has only one.”[^systems] If Amir wants to grow your business, lead people, make better decisions, build relationships, and become more confident, his body cannot be treated as disposable.

The first health target is not perfection. It is predictable energy. For the next 90 days, use a simple system.

| Health Lever | Amir 2.0 Standard | Minimum Standard |
|---|---|---|
| **Sleep** | Fixed sleep and wake window at least five nights per week. | Same wake time even after imperfect nights, with a short recovery plan. |
| **Training** | Three to four strength sessions weekly. | One set of push, pull, legs, or 10 minutes movement. |
| **Walking / Cardio** | 7,000–10,000 steps or 30 minutes walking most days. | 10-minute walk after a meal. |
| **Food** | Protein-centered meals, planned basics, fewer impulsive convenience choices. | One protein-first meal and water before caffeine or snacks. |
| **Nervous system** | Five minutes daily silence, breathing, prayer, meditation, or phone-free stillness. | Ten slow breaths before reacting. |
| **Body tracking** | Weekly weight, energy score, and training log. | One weekly check-in. |

The discipline-era resource emphasizes lowering activation energy.[^discipline] Prepare gym clothes, walking shoes, water, and the first task the night before. Do not make the future version fight avoidable friction. Amir 1.0 leaves everything to mood. Amir 2.0 sets the environment before the test begins.

## The Time and Attention System

Time blocking is where ambition becomes honest. If something matters but does not exist in the calendar, you are not committed yet. The ambitious-but-lazy resource is blunt: people who achieve their goals carve out and protect time.[^ambitious-lazy]

Amir needs three kinds of time blocks.

| Time Block | Purpose | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| **Deep Work Block** | Business strategy, offer creation, finance, learning, planning, or high-value execution. | No phone, no multitasking, one defined output. |
| **Operations Block** | business tasks, admin, staff communication, customer issues, logistics. | Batch reactive work instead of letting it leak everywhere. |
| **Recovery Block** | Sleep, training, walks, family, friends, silence, joy. | Recovery is scheduled because burnout is not a leadership strategy. |

Use a weekly calendar, not a vague to-do list. The week should contain protected blocks for your business growth, health, relationships, finance, and review. If the business schedule changes often, plan in anchors rather than exact perfection. For example, “first 60 minutes before opening,” “after lunch,” or “before going home” may work better than rigid times.

The rule is simple: **decide before the moment**. Decision fatigue kills execution. If you decide every day whether to train, whether to plan, whether to check finances, whether to create content, and whether to reach out to people, you will eventually negotiate your way back into Amir 1.0.

Amir 2.0 cannot be built in an Amir 1.0 environment. Dan Koe’s mind-game resource argues that the old self is stored in surroundings, feeds, routines, and social context.[^mind-game] Ali Abdaal’s systems resource adds that relationships need systems too, not because care should be mechanical, but because care should be reliable.[^systems]

Start with the environment.

| Bedroom | Entertainment and sleep mixed together. | Sleep-first environment; screens removed or controlled. |
| Customer/community space | Passive venue. | Intentional community board, event calendar, leaderboard, membership or return incentives. |

Then redesign relationships. Amir needs people who make Amir 2.0 easier to remember. This does not mean abandoning everyone. It means becoming more intentional about who receives time, whose opinions shape decisions, and who is invited into the created future.

| Relationship Category | Practical Standard |

| **Friends** | Initiate contact weekly. Do not wait until loneliness becomes heavy. |

Relationships are not only emotional support. They are part of the identity environment. If everyone around you expects Amir 1.0, Amir 2.0 must become visible through actions, words, and standards until the environment updates.

## The Momentum and Setback Protocol

Setbacks are guaranteed. The question is whether they become data or identity collapse. Dan Koe’s cybernetic model frames intelligence as setting a goal, acting, sensing feedback, comparing reality to the goal, and adjusting.[^fix-life] That is how Amir 2.0 handles setbacks. He does not dramatize. He steers.

| Setback | Amir 1.0 Interpretation | Amir 2.0 Protocol |
|---|---|---|
| Missed workout | “I failed again.” | Complete minimum version today and return to normal schedule tomorrow. |
| Weak business week | “The market is bad.” | Review inputs: offers, promotion, customer conversations, events, follow-up, numbers. |
| Broken promise | Hide, justify, or restart someday. | Clean it up within 24 hours: acknowledge, renegotiate, repair, recommit. |
| Low motivation | Wait for feeling to return. | Follow the minimum system; mood is not the manager. |
| Overwhelm | Avoid or consume more advice. | Brain dump, choose one next action, set 25-minute timer. |
| Criticism or failure | Make it personal. | Extract lesson, adjust, continue. |
| Falling behind | Quit because the plan is imperfect. | Reduce scope, protect the core habit, review on Sunday. |

Use the **24-hour reset rule**: never let one missed day become two without a deliberate response. If you break a habit, the next day is not a referendum on your character. It is a reset test. Amir 2.0 wins by returning faster.

Use the **minimum viable day** when life is chaotic. On difficult days, complete four things only: move for 10 minutes, write the top priority, take one your business action, and keep one relationship or cleanup promise. This prevents the old identity from using chaos as proof that standards are impossible.

A one-year vision is useful, but 90 days is where execution becomes visible. Use the quarterly quest model from the systems resource.[^systems] Each quarter should have one to three quests, not ten.

| **Week** | What are the few actions that matter now? | Weekly boss fight, calendar blocks, habit targets. |
| **Day** | What does Amir 2.0 do today? | Morning anchor, top three, minimum standards, shutdown. |

For the first 90 days, a strong version might look like this:

| Quest | 90-Day Outcome | Weekly Actions | Review Metric |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Quest 1: Rebuild Self-Trust** | Complete 80% or more of daily morning anchors and movement minimums. | Track habits daily; review every Sunday. | Habit completion percentage and self-trust score. |

This is ambitious enough to matter and simple enough to execute. Do not overcomplicate the first 90 days. The goal is not to become a finished man. The goal is to prove that Amir can install systems and produce evidence.

# Daily Operating System

This is the practical structure Amir follows after completing the three phases. Do not treat it as a perfect schedule. Treat it as the default operating system. Adjust times to your business’s real opening hours and responsibilities, but keep the sequence.

## Daily Structure

| Time / Moment | Operating Standard | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| **Wake-up** | Get out of bed, drink water, no phone. | Start as the owner of attention, not a consumer of inputs. |
| **First 10 minutes** | Silence, breathing, prayer, or stillness; then write today’s top three. | Regulate the nervous system and choose the day deliberately. |
| **Morning anchor** | Complete the first non-negotiable action before reactive work. | Build self-trust immediately. |
| **Body block** | Train, walk, stretch, or complete the 10-minute minimum. | Protect energy and identity. |
| **your business growth block** | Work 60–90 minutes on one strategic output: offer, event, system, finance, marketing, partnership, or customer insight. | Move from operator to builder. |
| **Operations block** | Handle business operations, messages, staff needs, admin, customer issues, and logistics in batches where possible. | Prevent reactive tasks from eating the entire day. |
| **Relationship touchpoint** | Send one meaningful message, invitation, thank-you, follow-up, or family check-in when scheduled. | Build connection intentionally. |
| **Learning block** | Study or practice one skill connected to the current 90-day quest. | Build real traits, not just motivation. |
| **Shutdown** | Review what happened, mark habits, choose tomorrow’s first task, clean the workspace, set phone boundaries. | End the day with closure and lower tomorrow’s activation energy. |
| **Sleep routine** | Screens down, environment quiet, consistent sleep window as often as possible. | Protect the next day before it begins. |

## Daily Non-Negotiables

These are the minimum standards. On excellent days, do more. On hard days, protect these.

| Non-Negotiable | Full Version | Minimum Version |
|---|---|---|
| **Move the body** | Full workout or 30-minute walk. | 10-minute walk or mobility. |
| **Move your business forward** | 60–90 minute growth block. | 20 minutes on one meaningful business action. |
| **Move the mind forward** | Journaling, learning, planning, or review. | Write one honest sentence and one next action. |
| **Keep one relationship alive** | Call, meet, invite, or deeply connect. | Send one thoughtful message when scheduled. |
| **Protect self-trust** | Complete the chosen 30-day habit. | Complete the tiny version before sleep. |

## Weekly Structure

The week is the basic unit of transformation. A strong day is useful, but a strong week changes identity.

| Weekly Practice | When | What to Do |
|---|---|---|
| **Sunday Weekly Review** | 45–60 minutes | Review goals, habits, your business numbers, energy, relationships, and lessons. Choose next week’s boss fight. |
| **your business Strategy Blocks** | 2–3 times weekly | Work on events, offers, partnerships, retention, content, systems, or finance. No reactive tasks. |
| **Finance Review** | Once weekly | Review business revenue, costs, cash, personal spending, debts, savings, and one decision. |
| **Training Plan** | 3–4 times weekly | Strength train, walk, or complete planned movement. Track completion. |
| **Relationship Block** | Once or twice weekly | Spend intentional time with family, friends, mentors, or community. |
| **Environment Reset** | Once weekly | Clean workspace, remove friction, prepare clothes, update calendar, reset phone boundaries. |
| **Adventure / Joy Block** | Once weekly | Do something that creates aliveness, not just productivity. Amir 2.0 is disciplined, not dead. |

During the Sunday review, answer five questions in writing. **What worked this week? What did not work? What did I avoid? What is the highest-leverage action next week? What promise must be made, kept, renegotiated, or cleaned up?**

## Monthly Structure

Each month is a checkpoint, not a restart. Do not reinvent the whole plan every month. Review, adjust, and continue.

| Monthly Review Area | Question |
|---|---|
| **Identity** | Am I acting more like Amir 2.0, or only thinking about him? |
| **Health** | Is my energy improving, declining, or staying the same? What is the cause? |
| **your business** | What experiment produced real feedback? What should be repeated, improved, or stopped? |
| **Money** | Are the numbers clearer than last month? What decision did clarity enable? |
| **Relationships** | Who did I invest in? Who did I neglect? What needs repair or initiation? |
| **Habits** | Which habit became easier? Which one needs a lower-friction system? |
| **Environment** | What in my physical or digital environment is still preserving Amir 1.0? |

At the end of each month, choose one 30-day focus for the next month. Keep the previous systems that are working, but do not overload the plan. The goal is compounding, not chaos.

## The Amir 2.0 Rules

These rules are not inspirational quotes. They are operating constraints.

| Rule | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **No vague ambition.** | Every goal needs a behavior, a calendar slot, and a review point. |
| **No broken word without cleanup.** | If you break a promise, acknowledge it, repair it, and recommit or renegotiate. |
| **No phone before self-command.** | The first part of the day belongs to Amir 2.0, not the algorithm. |
| **No strategy avoidance disguised as busyness.** | At least twice a week, work on your business’s future, not only its daily noise. |
| **No all-or-nothing resets.** | Missed days require return, not drama. |
| **No borrowed dreams without process acceptance.** | If you do not want the daily work, admit the desire is not yours or not current. |
| **No environment that keeps reinstalling Amir 1.0.** | Change the room, phone, feeds, calendar, and relationships until the new identity has support. |
| **No self-hatred as fuel.** | Standards are high, but the foundation is truth and respect. |

## Final Instruction

Amir 2.0 is not a future personality you wait to become. He is built through evidence. Every kept promise is evidence. Every workout is evidence. Every customer conversation is evidence. Every clean financial review is evidence. Every honest repair is evidence. Every strategic block for your business is evidence. Every time you pause before obeying the old impulse, you weaken Amir 1.0 and strengthen Amir 2.0.

Do not make this mystical. Do not make it complicated. Start with the next honest action. Then repeat.

> **Today, Amir 2.0 is not proven by how strongly you feel. He is proven by what you do next.**
