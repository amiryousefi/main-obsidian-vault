# Personal site samples — reference benchmarks

Five of the best-known personal advisor/consultant sites, reviewed July 2026
as calibration for the amiryousefi.com rebuild. Each is a different answer to
the same problem: one person selling judgment.

---

## 1. Jonathan Stark — [jonathanstark.com](https://jonathanstark.com)

The closest business model to Amir's. Value-priced consulting, no hourly
billing — his whole brand is "ditch the hourly rate" (literally the "I don't
sell hours" line on our site). Structure: one promise in the hero, a
testimonial immediately after, then a "Here's the problem…" narrative. Design
is plain — almost homely — and he charges premium anyway.

**Lesson:** offer clarity and a strong point of view outsell visual polish.

## 2. Tom Critchlow — [tomcritchlow.com](https://tomcritchlow.com)

The independent-consultant editorial. Sidebar index, conversational first
person ("Hey, I'm Tom"), writing front and center, projects below. His site
*is* his credibility — a decade of essays on independent consulting.

**Lesson:** this is what the site becomes in year two if the content engine
actually runs. Closest spirit to our `ivory-editorial` theme.

## 3. Dan Mall — [danmall.com](https://danmall.com)

Maximum art direction. Dark, giant type over florals, extremely designed.
Gorgeous — but he sells to *designers*, so the site must itself be a design
flex.

**Lesson:** this is `midnight-editorial`'s ceiling, and also its warning —
that level of drama makes sense only when aesthetics is the product. Ours
isn't; judgment is.

## 4. Derek Sivers — [sive.rs](https://sive.rs)

Radical trust minimalism. "Me in 10 seconds," dated life chapters, "I reply
to every email." Zero design in the conventional sense, total confidence.
The parenthetical at the top — "Everything here is 100% me, no AI" — is a
trust signal worth thinking about.

**Lesson:** personality compounds; templates don't.

## 5. Julian Shapiro — [julian.com](https://julian.com)

"The Handbooks Of." His homepage is literally an index of handbooks plus a
short About. This is our `operating-system` theme validated in the wild —
but he's selling *nothing* on the page; the handbook structure builds
audience, not bookings.

**Lesson:** keep the handbook concept as inspiration, not the storefront.

---

## The pattern across all five

- **Person first, always** — name, face or voice, first person. No corporate
  distance. All five would fail a "premium agency" test and that's exactly
  why they work.
- **One promise in one sentence** at the top. Stark: "make more and work
  less." Ours: "I turn complexity into direction."
- **Writing is the proof.** Every one leans on published thinking as the
  credibility engine. None relies on case studies alone — the content
  strategy matters more than any theme choice.
- **Decoration is inversely correlated with confidence.** Sivers and Stark,
  the two most established, have the plainest sites.

## The takeaway

`ivory-editorial` is already more visually premium than four of these five.
What every one of them has that we don't yet is **a living body of written
thinking and visible price/offer confidence**. The gap isn't design — it's
the publishing habit.
