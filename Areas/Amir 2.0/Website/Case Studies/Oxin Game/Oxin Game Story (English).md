# My Story (English)

Part of the Oxin Game case. The Farsi note is still empty.

Oxin runs game clubs. Customers pay for time on high-spec machines. The regulars are Gen Z players who come back weekly, and there is more than one branch. The doors are open almost always. A venue that runs all day and all night has no maintenance window. That was the constraint I came in under.

I came in as a consultant. The case note calls the work product and systems design. The CV files it under advisory. The scope was the operational architecture, the data model for customers and sessions, and the engagement features on top. The same place was already running several other projects. The notes do not say when I started, who asked me in, or whether the work is finished.

What I found was a live business running on tools that could not see across branches. Staff worked one branch at a time. Pricing was set by hand. Nobody had a structured memory of who played what, how often, and when they stopped coming. For a venue that lives on repeat visits, that last gap is the expensive one. Retention sat with whoever happened to be at the counter.

A 24/7 operation sets the constraint before any feature does. The platform had to be safe to change while a customer was mid-session. Deployment, monitoring, and what happens on failure came first. The notes do not record how a deploy actually behaved.

The machine-management layer already worked. The early bullet calls it iCafe. The finished case, the index, and the CV call it iCafeCloud. Replacing it would have taken months and created two sources of truth. So the new platform inherited that foundation and spent the build on what was missing. A view across branches. And a customer record that was a history, not a receipt.

Transactions, sessions, and play history had to be one record. That is what makes a retention action possible. An early bullet also says AI was used to analyse how people behave and how they spend money in the venue. The finished case does not repeat that sentence, so I am not treating it as shipped.

AI showed up in two places. Pricing, so the price could respond to demand instead of sitting in a static table. And development, so a place with several projects could keep pace without expanding the team. The notes do not say which model, which workflow, or what the headcount was.

What went in was one operational view for every branch, live monitoring, a dashboard of daily transactions, AI-assisted pricing, a CRM tied to play history, and iCafeCloud kept rather than thrown out. Branch-by-branch guesswork became one live picture. Anonymous repeat traffic became people the business can recognise. Delivery kept moving without a proportional increase in headcount.

I am not going to invent a failure. The notes do not record an outage or a decision I would reverse.

Three figures are still open before this should be published. How many branches. Whether retention moved after the CRM. Whether the pricing changed revenue. The case note says one real number would carry more weight than the rest of that section. Public naming of Oxin is also still a launch check, not a cleared fact.

The lesson I would keep is the one already in the case. Integrate with what already works, and spend the build on the gap nobody else is covering.
