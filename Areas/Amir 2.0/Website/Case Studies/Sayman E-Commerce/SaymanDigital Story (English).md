English version of [[SaymanDigital Story (Farsi)]].

I joined the Sayman Digital team at three different points in time.
The first time was when their website was still being developed on WordPress and hadn't launched yet. It was because of the WordPress problems they had, and also how development was being done.
The initial idea was to build a custom system for them from scratch. My role was the initial design of this system and the software roadmap. Also making sure they would get a proper outcome.
They had a WordPress developer who was very slow and didn't deliver the output they needed.
They also weren't using git or proper development tools. This first time I joined them for consulting work was in 2015. Given how costly development was and that they were just starting out, we hired a more specialized WordPress developer for them. Within a short time, a version of this e-commerce store was launched using WordPress. They then moved toward writing a custom theme and the custom plugins needed to function as a complete specialty store.


In 2019 I joined again. The WordPress version had turned into a mature, custom build. As a regional store it had around 20,000 active users, with a product list of over 5,000 items, processing around a hundred orders a day.
Now was the time for WordPress to show its own problems. The solution that had once been the best option for this company, and had produced the best results for it, had now become a brake on their progress.
Sayman Digital had now become an established company. It had stable revenue. The first time I'd joined this company, our concerns were about how to get started, launch quickly, and build it. Now we had to look at it with the mindset that all operations — supply, warehousing, sales, support, and so on — would be facilitated by developing new software.
As a first step, we started documenting the company. Also, given that the company had different departments, we decided to set up an ERP system for the company. The development team, using the project-management module, was the first team to start using the ERP. The plan was for the rest of the company's departments to gradually start using it too, so we could go on to integrate all of the company's processes.
To start, we documented the previous system and all the plugins and custom code that had been written inside WordPress.
We also looked at large e-commerce systems like Magento and other custom storefront scripts, like the ones built on Laravel.
We put together a plan of all the subsystems the store needed. We tried to spot the blind spots. We put together a few rough estimates so we could assess the project's feasibility.
We wanted to have all the features currently built on WordPress. We wanted to improve the system's speed and efficiency. We also wanted to be ready for exponential growth in users, products, and orders.
The marketing and content team was performing well. The company's Instagram page had reached around 120,000 followers, and the company was gearing up to run high-traffic marketing campaigns with strong sales.
The new system also had to be designed so it would sync with the company's different departments and integrate our data and processes.
Building a new system from scratch wasn't a fancy exercise, or about writing better code for its own sake. It was about building a product that served the business and its customers.
Even though we'd started the work in person, COVID and quarantine began partway through. Having clear processes and working based on documentation inside the ERP project-management system meant we were fully ready for remote work during that period. Still, building and enabling communication during that time was a cultural shift for the team that affected everyone.
Alongside developing the new system, we were also responsible for maintaining the previous WordPress system. Fortunately, our team included the earlier developers as well. Also, by documenting the previous system, we'd decided which areas we would maintain and which improvements we'd let go of.
COVID caused online shopping to grow more, which meant the current system was being used more. We tried to keep our development capacity in service of the business goals — keeping the current system running at its best, both in terms of user experience and in terms of speed and SEO, while putting more development capacity toward building the new system.
