## Full case

### Client context

Asr'e Ahan — a business that had reached the point where it needed its own
software capability, without an existing engineering organisation to build on.

*Unverified: industry, company size, engagement dates, and whether the
engagement is complete.*

### Initial challenge

The company faced the problem every non-technical business hits at this
threshold: it needed to hire developers, choose a stack, and set up a way of
working — while having no internal reference point for judging any of those
three decisions.

Get the sequence wrong and the cost compounds. A team hired against a vague
standard, working on an ill-fitting stack, with no shared development flow,
produces software that becomes someone else's rescue project within a year.

### Role and scope

Advisor. No implementation — the deliverables were decisions, standards, and
documents the client's own team would run on.

This is the engagement shape that most closely matches how I work now:
strategic direction for a team that will execute it themselves.

### Strategic decisions

**1. Fix the hiring standard before the hiring.**
What roles, at what level, in what order, judged against what bar. Hiring is
the highest-leverage decision a new engineering function makes and the hardest
one to reverse.

**2. Choose a stack the client can hire into and maintain.**
Stack selection for a first team is a staffing decision as much as a technical
one. The right answer is the one that has a local talent pool and will not
strand the business in two years.

**3. Write the development flow down before the team forms.**
Git workflow and development process, documented up front — so practice comes
from a written standard rather than from whoever happens to join first.

**4. Leave a handbook, not a dependency.**
The developer handbook was the core deliverable. A handbook keeps working
after the advisor leaves; verbal advice does not.

### Execution highlights

- **Development team hiring** — consulting on roles, sequence, and evaluation.
- **Developer handbook** — written standards and practices for the new team.
- **Git and software development flow** — branching, review, and delivery
  process defined before the team scaled.
- **Tech stack consulting** — technology selection matched to the business and
  its hiring market.

### Lasting impact

The deliverables were designed to be self-sustaining: a hiring standard, a
stack decision, and a written flow all keep operating without the advisor in
the room.

### What this proves

Not every engagement needs my hands on the code. Where the real bottleneck is
hiring, standards, and process, the highest-value contribution is the decision
framework — and the documentation that makes it stick.
